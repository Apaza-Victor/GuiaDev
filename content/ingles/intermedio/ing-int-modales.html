Habilidad y Permiso: Can, Could, Be Able To

Objetivo

Los modales no se conjugan (nunca llevan -s, ni to delante): dominarlos multiplica tu expresividad con solo 10 palabras.

Habilidad

Respuesta a "Would you mind...?": para decir que sí, di "No problem" o "Not at all". Un "Yes" literal suena a que sí te molesta.

Obligación: Must, Have To, Should

Los tres niveles de obligación

NivelModalEjemplo Escuchar
Obligación fuerte / leymust / have toYou must wear a helmet. I have to work on Saturdays
Recomendaciónshould / shouldn'tYou should backup your data. You shouldn't skip breakfast
Sin obligacióndon't have toYou don't have to come — it's optional
Prohibiciónmustn'tYou mustn't share the API key

Must vs Mustn't vs Don't Have To

Trío que confunde a todos:

  • must = es necesario hacerlo → Students must pass the exam.
  • mustn't = está prohibido → You mustn't smoke here.
  • don't have to = es opcional → You don't have to dress up.
Matiz: must = obligación interna o del hablante ("I must finish this"); have to = obligación externa ("I have to wear a uniform"). En el pasado usa had to (must no tiene pasado).

Probabilidad: May, Might, Could

La escalera de certeza

CertezaExpresiónEjemplo Escuchar
100%willThe sun will rise tomorrow
~90%must (deducción)He's yawning. He must be tired
~50%may / might / couldIt might rain. She may know the answer
~10%might not / may notThey might not come
0% (deducción)can'tThat can't be true!

En conversación real

  • It could work, but we need more testing.
  • We might launch next week if QA passes.
  • He may have missed the email (= quizás lo perdió, en pasado).
Maybe (quizás) va al inicio de la frase y NO es modal: Maybe he's right = He might be right.

Deducciones: Must, Can't, Might

Detective mode: sacar conclusiones por evidencia

EvidenciaDeducciónEstructura Escuchar

He answered wrongHe can't have studiedcan't be/have = seguro que no
No reply yetShe might be busymight/may be/have = posible

Deducciones sobre el pasado

modal + have + participio:

  • The ground is wet. It must have rained.
  • She can't have finished already — the exam is 3 hours!
  • They might have forgotten about the meeting.

Práctica

Deduce la situación:

  1. John's car isn't here. He ___ (leave). (→ must have left)
  2. That ___ be Maria — she's in Brazil! (imposible → can't)
  3. I'm not sure where Anna is; she ___ (be) in a meeting. (→ might be)
