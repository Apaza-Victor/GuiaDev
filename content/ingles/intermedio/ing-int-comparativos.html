Comparativos y Superlativos

Objetivo

Comparar cualquier cosa con precisión: precios, tecnologías, candidatos, opciones.

Reglas de formación

AdjetivoComparativoSuperlativo
cortos (1 sílaba): cheapcheaper thanthe cheapest
terminan en -y: easyeasier thanthe easiest
largos (2+ sílabas): expensivemore expensive thanthe most expensive
irregulares: good / bad / farbetter / worse / furtherthe best / the worst / the furthest

Estructuras frecuentes

  • Much/far + comparative: This laptop is much faster than mine.
  • (not) as ... as: Vue is as popular as React here. It's not as hard as it looks.
  • The more..., the more...: The more you practice, the better you get.
  • by far / one of the: Rust is one of the fastest languages.
Error típico: ❌ more better, ❌ the most easiest. Nunca combines MORE con -er ni MOST con -est.

Cláusulas Relativas: Who, Which, That

Unir dos frases en una más rica

Los relativos reemplazan a un sustantivo y conectan información:

PronombreSe usa paraEjemplo
who / thatpersonasThe dev who wrote this left the company
which / thatcosasThe tool which saved us hours is free
wherelugaresThe office where I worked is closed now
whoseposesiónThe guy whose laptop was stolen got a new one
whentiemposI remember the day when we shipped v1

Omite who/which/that cuando es OBJETO

The movie (that) we saw was great.   → objeto: se puede omitir
The guy who called you is my boss.   → sujeto: NO se puede omitir
En inglés americano, that es preferido para personas y cosas en cláusulas definitorias; which sin coma solo suena británico.

Defining vs Non-Defining

La coma lo cambia todo

TipoFunciónEjemplo
Defining (sin comas)identifica CUÁL exactamente; es esencialMy sister who lives in Rome is a chef (= tengo otra hermana en otra parte)
Non-defining (con comas)agrega dato extra prescindibleMy sister, who lives in Rome, is a chef (= hija única, el dato es extra)

Reglas del non-defining

  • Nunca uses that: ✅ Paris, which is in France... ❌ Paris, that is...
  • Nunca omitas el pronombre: ✅ The Eiffel Tower, which we visited, was amazing.
  • Puede referirse a TODA la frase anterior: He passed the exam, which surprised everyone.

Práctica

Une las frases con un relativo:

  1. I met a girl. Her father works at Google. → ...
  2. This is the app. I told you about it. → ...
  3. Lima has great food. Lima is Peru's capital. → ...

Solución: whose father works · about which I told you / that I told you about · Lima, which is Peru's capital, has...
