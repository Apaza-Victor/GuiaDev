Rango Idiomático

Decir lo mismo con creciente sofisticación

BásicoNaturalSofisticado

It's very importantIt's crucial / keyIt's paramount / pivotal
I don't understandI'm lost / I didn't catch thatThat went right over my head
He's very smartHe's sharp / brightHe's razor-sharp / quick on the uptake
It was difficultIt was tough / demandingIt was an uphill battle / no picnic
We solved itWe figured it outWe cracked it / ironed out the kinks

Intensificadores que no son "very"

very tired     → utterly exhausted · dead tired
very happy     → thrilled · over the moon
very angry     → furious · livid · fuming
very big       → massive · enormous · huge
very small     → tiny · minuscule
very good      → outstanding · top-notch · stellar
very bad       → awful · dreadful · abysmal
Cuidado con el registro: "paramount" y "stellar" brillan en escritura; "dead tired" y "over the moon" viven en el habla. Mezclar registros es el error clásico del avanzado.

Práctica

Sube el registro de estas frases dos niveles:

  1. "The demo was very good and the client was very happy."
  2. "The migration was very hard but we solved it."

Malas noticias con tacto

"I'm afraid we have some bad news."
"Unfortunately, it won't be possible after all."
"We regret to inform you that..."        (formal)
"I hate to be the bearer of bad news, but..."
"Let's agree to disagree."               (cerrar desacuerdo)
"It's not you, it's the budget."         (rechazo laboral)

Fórmulas sociales imprescindibles

  • Aceptando una invitación: "I'd love to!" Rechazándola: "I'd love to, but I can't make it."
  • Cortesía al interrumpir: "Sorry to jump in..." / "If I may..."
  • Al irte temprano: "I hate to run, but..."
  • Regalo no gustado: "It's the thought that counts."
El eufemismo NO es hipocresía: es cortesía estructural. Llamar "fired" a alguien recién despedido en inglés corporativo suena cruel; "let go" es lo esperado.
