A vs An vs The

Objetivo

El artículo es el error #1 del hispanohablante porque en español funciona distinto. Estas reglas cierran ese hueco para siempre.

A / AN: uno cualquiera, primera mención

ReglaEjemplo Escuchar
a + sonido consonantea book, a university (suena "yu-"), a European
an + sonido vocalan apple, an hour (la h es muda), an MBA (suena "em")
ProfesionesShe is a developer / an engineer
Primera vez en la conversaciónI saw a movie last night

THE: ya identificado, único o compartido

  • Ya mencionado: I saw a movie. The movie was great.
  • Único en su contexto: the sun, the kitchen, the boss (= nuestro jefe).
  • Especificado por lo que sigue: the book you lent me, the best option.
  • Con superlativos SIEMPRE: the fastest, the most expensive.

Zero article: SIN artículo

CasosEjemplos Escuchar
Sustantivos plurales genéricosDogs are loyal. Developers love coffee
Incontables abstractosMoney, time, information, advice, software
Comidas y idiomasafter lunch, speak Spanish
La mayoría de países y ciudadesPeru, Lima, Japan — pero the US, the Netherlands, the UK
Trampa clásica: "information" e "advice" son INCONTABLES en inglés: ✅ some information, a piece of advice . ❌ ~~informations~~, ~~advices~~.

Zero Article en Detalle

Los casos que más fallan

Sin artículo ❌ con theCorrecto Escuchar
go to ~~the~~ bed / school / work / churchcomo institución-rutina va sin the: go to bed, go to work
~~The~~ life is hard / ~~the~~ technology changes fastconcepto general abstracto: life is hard
next ~~the~~ year / last ~~the~~ weeknext year, last week (¡pero "the next day" en narración!)
at ~~the~~ homeat home
by ~~the~~ car / ~~the~~ busby car, by bus (medio de transporte)

El mismo sustantivo, dos mundos

She's in school        → es estudiante (institución)
She's in THE school    → está dentro del edificio
He's in prison         → preso
He's in THE prison     → visitando
What time do you finish work?  → tu jornada
THE work you did was excellent → el producto específico

Práctica rápida

Pon a/an/the/— :

  1. ___ honesty is ___ best policy. (— / the)
  2. She wants to be ___ engineer. (an)
  3. ___ coffee in Brazil is amazing. (The — específico) vs ___ coffee keeps me awake. (— genérico)
  4. We went to ___ bed early after ___ long flight. (— / a)

There Is / There Are

Existencia: "hay"

FormaUsoEjemplo Escuchar
There issingular / incontableThere is a bug in prod. There's some milk left
There arepluralThere are two options. There aren't any tickets
Is there...? / Are there...?preguntasIs there a pharmacy near here?

En otros tiempos

  • Pasado: There was / There were — There were three errors in the log.
  • Futuro: There will be — There will be a meeting at 3.
  • Perfecto: There has been / have been — There have been complaints about the new UI.
  • Modal: There must/could/might be — There might be an issue with caching.

Errores típicos

❌ Español mental✅ Inglés real Escuchar
~~It has a problem with~~There is a problem with the build
~~Is possible that...~~It is possible that... (posibilidad = it)
There are ~~much~~ peopleThere are many / a lot of people
There is ~~many~~ bugsThere are several bugs
Regla de concordancia con listas: concuerda con el PRIMER elemento: "There's a laptop, two phones and a camera in the bag." (nativo aceptado).

Preguntas y Negaciones con Do

Do-support: el auxiliar que el español no tiene

Todos los verbos normales necesitan DO para preguntar y negar en presente/pasado simple:

AfirmativoNegativoPregunta Escuchar
You like itYou don't like itDo you like it?
She works hereShe doesn't work hereDoes she work here?
They went homeThey didn't go homeDid they go home?
Doble error típico: ❌ She doesn't works (el -s ya se lo llevó does) y ❌ Did you went? (did absorbe el pasado; verbo en forma base). El auxiliar toma la conjugación; el verbo queda desnudo.

Excepciones: verbos que NO usan do

BE invierte solo: Are you tired? She isn't tired. Los modales (can, will, should...) también: Can you help? I can't help. Y have got: Have you got a pen? (UK) — aunque "Do you have a pen?" es igual de válido (US).

Preguntas de sujeto vs de objeto

Sujeto (quién hizo):   WHO called you?      → SIN do
Objeto (qué hizo):      What DID you call?   → CON do

Who broke the window?       ✓ (who = sujeto)
What did he break?          ✓ (what = objeto)

Práctica final

Corrige estos errores:

  1. Does she likes her job?
  2. Why you didn't came to standup?
  3. Who did invent the internet?
